For our clients

Choosing cabinetry is mostly a question of who will actually turn up.

That is almost impossible to judge from photographs. It is very easy to judge from someone who has been through it.

The part that is hard to see

People comparing cabinetry are rarely comparing finishes. They are trying to work out who will measure properly, who will hold a timeline, and who will come back when a door settles after a few months.

None of that is visible in a photograph. It is obvious to someone who has lived with the work.

What we are asking

If someone you know is at that stage, introduce us. Not a recommendation — an introduction, so they can judge for themselves.

We will start where the project should start: their space, their storage, and how they actually use the room. And we will tell them plainly if the lead time does not suit what they need.

What they receive

A full design consultation

Their layout, storage zones, appliance placement and daily use reviewed properly before any material or finish is discussed. If the timeline or the scope is wrong for them, we say so at that meeting rather than later.

What you receive

  • Your project, published — with your permission and on your terms. Photographed properly, credited, and yours to share.
  • Senior design hours for the next room, whenever you get to it.
  • First look at new materials and hardware before they reach the showroom floor.

We do not pay for introductions. A fee would change what the recommendation means, both for you and for the person receiving it.

A note for designers and architects

If you specify cabinetry professionally, we do not offer you a commission and we will not.

Your Code requires that you are compensated by your client alone. What we offer instead is the part that costs you time: drawings in the format you issue, honest lead times given once, and installation dates set against your programme — not ours.
