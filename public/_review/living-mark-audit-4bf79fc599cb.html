Living rooms are the worst category for third-party IP, and there's a reason. A living room is where a catalogue stylist puts the media wall, the bookshelf and the display cabinet — every one of those is a surface for somebody else's brand. All six failures are posters, figurines, a magazine and shop signage. Not one is a supplier logo on the cabinetry itself.

What this unlocks — and the gap

The clean set is unusually coherent: nearly all TV/media walls and lit display cabinetry, across three schemes (green classic, dark contemporary, pale minimal). That's enough distinct reveals for a full reel with nothing generated.

The gap: there are no clean close-up mechanism cuts — no drawer, no lift-up, no pull-out at detail scale. Every clean render is a room-wide elevation, which is the losing reel's all-wide profile. Pair these with the clean Storage Systems renders, or generate the detail cuts from a clean master.

Three things to add to the standing method

  • The media wall is a poster wall. Posters, game art, vinyl sleeves and character figurines are the styling in a living-room render, not background.
  • Supplier showroom photographs are mixed in with the renders. The tell is a pure-white border on all four corners — a catalogue page scan, not CGI.
  • A branded laptop is a mark. The Apple logo on a MacBook lid shows up in four renders here, mid-desk, where nobody thinks to look.
One correction, mine. I flagged a Windows watermark on a screen in B720_C26_p019 off the low-res triage sheet. At native resolution it's a framed nebula photograph and the “text” was its star field. A triage candidate isn't a finding until it's re-cut at full size.
