The prompt, identical across all three

ARRI Alexa 35, Zeiss Supreme Prime 40mm at T2.8, Kodak Vision3 250D. A woman pulling open a deep pan drawer in a walnut kitchen, honed grey stone worktop, matte black handleless pulls. Soft north window light from camera left, late morning. Natural skin texture, visible pores, no retouching. Subtle 35mm grain, mild halation. No lettering or logos anywhere.

Soul Cinema

BEST FILM TEXTURE — FAILS THE HAND

Genuinely filmic: real grain structure, warm falloff, deep shadows that hold detail. This is the look you were pointing at. But it ignored the brief — no wide shot, no person, it went straight to a macro. And the hand is a hard fail.

Native-resolution check

The hand at 2×. Fingers fused into a single mass, no knuckle definition, no nails, thumb merged into the palm. This is the classic failure — and it lands on the exact thing our reels need: a hand on hardware.

Soul 2.0

MOST CONVINCING AS A PHOTOGRAPH — INVENTED FILM BRANDING

The most believable of the three as an actual photo. Skin is not plasticky, hair has flyaways, the shirt has real fabric behaviour, the grade is muted and filmic. It also ignored the cabinet spec — shaker fronts and bar handles instead of flat handleless.

Native-resolution check

At 3×, mirrored film edge-code printed into the scene: “S A 3.1 8I8I8 E”. This is Kodak film rebate marking. Naming the stock made the model draw the stock’s own branding — inside the frame, on the drawer box.

Nano Banana

BEST PRODUCT GEOMETRY — INVENTED HARDWARE BRANDING

By far the best cabinetry: a correctly constructed metal drawer box with sides and runners, pans inside, a plausible edge pull. This is the one that understood the product. Weaker skin, and it came back at a lower resolution than the other two.

Native-resolution check

At 4×, two embossed lettering blocks stamped into the drawer box — exactly where Blum and Hettich put their marks on a real box. Not fully legible, which by our own rule is not a pass.

What the test actually settled

  • Naming real bodies and glass works. Soul Cinema’s grain, halation and shadow falloff are genuinely filmic — better than anything we get from “cinematic” as a word. Your instinct was right.
  • But naming the film stock backfires. “Kodak Vision3 250D” made Soul 2.0 paint Kodak’s edge-code into the scene. Name the body and lens; describe grain by look, never by stock name.
  • All three ignored a blacklist. The prompt said “No lettering or logos anywhere” and two of three invented lettering regardless. Third independent confirmation that whitelisting beats blacklisting — now with our own data.
  • The split is clean and it matches the architecture. Nano Banana understood the product. Soul understood the photograph. Neither did both. That is the argument for compositing a real product into a generated room, not asking one model for both.
  • Stills cost nothing. 0.41 credits each. There is no reason to ever skip the still gate before spending 45 on a clip.
