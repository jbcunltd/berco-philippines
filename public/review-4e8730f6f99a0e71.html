The mark gate, at native resolution

✅ THE WHITELIST WORKED

“BERCO” engraved into the stone edge — cleanly formed, correctly spelled, properly lit. The model was told our mark was the only lettering permitted, and it placed it and invented nothing else. It is a stencil face, not our actual wordmark, so this frame cannot ship as-is. But the mechanism is proven.

✅ AND IT WORKED ON THE EXACT SURFACE THAT FAILED BEFORE

The drawer box in this morning’s test carried invented embossed lettering, right where Blum and Hettich stamp theirs. Same model, same surface, whitelist instead of blacklist — and it now reads “Berco” with a mark glyph. That is a clean A/B on one variable.

✅ THIRD CONFIRMATION

Nano Banana again, on the pull. Malformed letterforms, but unmistakably our name rather than an invented brand.

❌ THE ONE FAILURE — AND IT IS INSTRUCTIVE

Magazines inside the drawer carrying mirrored gibberish — “ᴎÑngoɿ”, “nƍɒoᙠ ᙠuᴉɒoǫb” — plus an invented face illustration and a landscape photograph. The whitelist governs which mark is allowed. It does not stop the model populating a scene with printed objects, and printed objects want text. The fix is a companion rule that excludes the object class: no printed matter, packaging, books, magazines, labels or signage in frame.

What this settled

  • The whitelist works, and it is the headline. Three of four frames that rendered any mark rendered ours. This morning, under a blacklist, two of three invented one. Same models, one variable changed.
  • It fails on printed objects, not on surfaces. Hardware, stone and cabinet faces obeyed. A stack of magazines did not. Add a companion line excluding printed matter, packaging, labels and signage.
  • Wides are unreliable; detail and human-scale are dependable. Both architectural wides ignored the specified lens and camera position. Every close and human shot followed it. Reels live at detail and human scale, so this is survivable — but we cannot prompt an establishing shot.
  • Nano Banana still wins the product. Frame 8 is the best of the eight: correct drawer box, real sides, plausible runner. Soul owns texture; Nano Banana owns geometry.
  • Colour drifts cold on wides. Frames 1 and 2 lost the walnut warmth. The sampled-colour anchor is still needed.
  • Cost: 2.72 credits for eight 2K frames. Roughly 0.34 each. The still gate is effectively free.
