Scroll motion — sample
Motion study

Scroll, but quietly.

Every movement here is native CSS. No animation library, no JavaScript driving the scroll, nothing hijacking your thumb. Flip the switch above to feel the difference.

Your browser doesn't support scroll-driven CSS yet. Notice that everything is still perfectly readable — that's the point. The motion is an enhancement, never a requirement.
01 — Entry

Fade and rise

Content lifts about 15px as it enters. Enough to feel considered; small enough that reading is never delayed. This is the workhorse — it goes on section headings and paragraphs.

02 — Reveal

Image unmask

The photograph uncovers from the top and settles out of a slight over-scale. It reads like a curtain rather than a pop.

03 — Sticky hero

The restrained parallax

The panel holds while the image drifts slowly behind it. This is the tasteful version of the effect — no dissolving portraits, no WebGL.

04 — Stagger

A grid that arrives in order

Each tile animates against its own position, so they arrive in sequence naturally. No JS timers, no orchestration.

Why this way

It cannot stutter

These animations run on the compositor, off the main thread. A scroll library like GSAP or Lenis adds 30–100KB of JavaScript and runs handlers on the main thread — which is where stutter comes from on a mid-range phone.

Nothing moves that you need to read first. Motion frames the content; it never delays it. And anyone with "reduce motion" switched on in their phone settings sees this page completely still — automatically.
